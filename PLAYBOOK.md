# AToure x Rolling Stone Africa: creator project playbook

How the Janet Jackson in Côte d'Ivoire proposal was built, written so the same
process can be repeated for another creator in a separate chat and repository.

Paste this file, or the sections you need, into the new chat as opening context.

---

## 1. What this engagement is

Rolling Stone Africa runs a documentary series called Culture on Culture. They
invite a globally known figure to a African country, film an episode, and pair it
with a Rolling Stone Africa cover story. They approached AToure to deliver the
visit on the ground in Côte d'Ivoire.

AToure sits as project and tour manager: institutional access, protocol,
security, logistics, hospitality, permits and local sponsorship. Rolling Stone
Africa owns the editorial product and the relationship with the artist's team.

Competition exists. Other entities want the same mandate, so speed and clarity
of proposal matter.

---

## 2. Commercial structure, the part to get right first

AToure earns in two separate streams:

1. **Base organisation fee.** Preferred as a percentage of the finalised total
   tour budget, targeted around twenty five percent. If the finalised budget
   comes in too low for a percentage to be worthwhile, this becomes a flat fee
   instead. The number cannot be set until Rolling Stone Africa confirms the
   tour budget.
2. **Sponsorship commission.** A percentage of sponsorship revenue AToure
   personally brings into the project. Percentage still to be negotiated.

**The single most important distinction.** The budget deck Rolling Stone Africa
sends is *their* projection of what the tour costs. It covers artist side items
such as glam team, assistants and travel. It is not a proposal for AToure's fee
and must never be read as one. Keep the two separated in every document.

**Never print the fee percentages in the client facing proposal.** They are a
negotiating position for the call, not published material. The proposal names
the two streams and how they are structured, nothing more.

## 3. The process, phase by phase

### Phase 1: read the brief properly
Rolling Stone Africa sends a concept deck, usually a PDF in French. Extract the
text and read all of it. Then judge what it actually is. In the Janet case it was
a concept pitch, not a production brief, so it was missing everything an operator
needs.

Standard gaps to look for and raise:
1. Confirmation status. Has the artist's team actually agreed. Has the
   traditional authority or venue agreed. Any government sign off yet.
2. Dates. Concept decks routinely contain none.
3. AToure's scope. Artist logistics, production logistics, or both.
4. Budget, and who pays whom.
5. Rights: footage and image ownership, approval rights, cancellation terms.
6. Track record of the series itself. Is this a pilot or an established format.

### Phase 2: establish position before building anything
Confirm AToure's role, who signs the agreement, and the deal stage. Write the
fee structure down. Everything downstream depends on this.

### Phase 3: build the evidence base once, reuse it forever
Pull the verified case study numbers from atoureconsulting.com and save them into
the project repository as markdown under `reference/`. This stops repeated
scraping and gives every later document one source of truth.

The three proof points, all figures verified from the live site:

**IShowSpeed x Côte d'Ivoire, 2024.** 55.8M Instagram views, 10M YouTube views,
15 state visas, over 600 security personnel mobilised citywide, sponsors MTN,
Solibra and Snedai closed from zero pre existing relationships, personal
recognition from the Minister of Tourism. This is the strongest card for any
Ivorian project because it is the same market.

**IShowSpeed x Morocco, AFCON Final 2025.** AToure conceived the official mascot
integration and secured CAF and Royal Moroccan Football Federation sign off. 16M
live stream views, a tournament record 6.1 billion digital impressions, 97.3M TV
audience for the final.

**Ashton Hall Africa Tour, 2026.** Five countries, Ghana, Cameroon, Nigeria,
Benin and Rwanda. 1.57 billion total tour views, biggest single post 171M, press
in every market in three languages.

### Phase 4: formal acceptance letter
A short letter from Baba Touré, Director, that accepts the invitation, introduces
AToure, cites the three proof points, ties the firm to why this specific project
matters, and asks for a call on terms. Kept qualitative where a figure is
disputed.

### Phase 5: the proposal itself
Built as an interactive web page rather than a PDF, published on the AToure
domain. It is faster to update, works on a phone, and can be sent as a link.

### Phase 6: test properly before sending
See the technical section below. A proposal that renders blank on the recipient's
phone is worse than no proposal.

### Phase 7: publish
Merge and deploy to the live site. Confidential pages carry a noindex tag and stay
out of the sitemap.

## 4. Anatomy of the proposal page, reuse this order

The section order was chosen so the reader meets emotion first, doubt second, and
proof third. It worked well and should be reused.

1. **Hero.** Creator name, country, the one line positioning, and the chapter
   chips naming each location.
2. **The Story.** The historical or emotional hook, told as a then and now
   timeline. For Janet: Michael Jackson received by the Royal House of Sanwi at
   Krindjabo in 1992, Janet returning more than three decades later. Every
   creator needs its own version of this. Find the genuine link.
3. **Why AToure.** Four pillars: direct institutional access, security at
   national scale, sponsors already closed in this market, and receipts rather
   than promises. Follow it with a side by side proof image.
4. **Strategic Access Network.** Filterable cards covering the Presidency, the
   Office of the First Lady, the ministries, the wider ministerial network,
   Interior and the National Police, the Greater Abidjan municipalities, aviation
   and commercial partners. This is the section competitors cannot copy. Always
   close it with the disclaimer that access is not a confirmed commitment.
5. **Track Record.** The three case studies with animated figures, photo strips
   that open in a lightbox, and links to the full case study pages.
6. **The Journey.** Interactive day tabs for the itinerary. State plainly that
   this is a concept itinerary and the exact programme will be organised and
   created with the Rolling Stone Africa team and the tour producers. That
   sentence protects the relationship.
7. **A timing hook.** For Janet this was the Children of Africa Gala on
   9 October 2026 at the Sofitel Abidjan Hôtel Ivoire, founded by First Lady
   Dominique Ouattara, past editions gathering more than 900 personalities
   including President Ouattara, Nicolas Sarkozy, Isabelle Adjani and Youssou
   N'Dour. AToure can pursue an honorary guest invitation through the Office of
   the First Lady. Find the equivalent fixed date for the next creator: it turns
   urgency into something institutional rather than desperate.
8. **Operations and Security.** Six blocks covering protocol, visas, close
   protection, transport, hospitality, permits and media.
9. **Budget.** The Rolling Stone Africa figures presented cleanly with a FCFA and
   USD toggle, the recommended working envelope, and every assumption and
   exclusion listed openly.
10. **Engagement.** The two fee streams described without percentages.
11. **Call to action.** Framed around institutional calendars, not around
    competitors.

## 5. Hard rules

1. **No hyphens or dashes anywhere in copy.** House rule from CLAUDE.md.
   Restructure the sentence instead. Check this automatically before publishing.
2. **Never publish an unverified figure.** Ask rather than estimate. Where a
   number is disputed, describe it qualitatively instead. The AToure site
   currently states three sponsors on the Ivory Coast case study page and two on
   the Our Work summary tile. That discrepancy is still unresolved, so the
   proposal names MTN, Solibra and Snedai rather than giving a count.
3. **Keep fee percentages out of client facing documents.**
4. **Label reach and impressions as estimated** wherever they appear publicly.
5. **Use the brand kit in the repository.** `brand-kit/01-logos/atoure/` holds a
   true vector nav lockup in on light and on dark versions. Colours are specified
   in `brand-kit/03-colours/colours.txt`: Gold `#C8A951`, Gold Light `#E0C47A`,
   Gold Dark `#A08830`, Gold Pale `#F5EDD6`, Black `#0D0C0A`, Black Soft
   `#1A1814` for panels, Cream `#FAF6EE`, Warm White `#FEFCF8`, Text Mid
   `#5C5040`, Text Light `#9A8870`. Fonts are Cormorant Garamond for display and
   Jost for everything else.
6. **French matters.** Ivorian stakeholders read French. The Janet proposal has a
   full EN and FR toggle covering every section including the day panels and the
   budget. Build it bilingual from the start rather than retrofitting.

## 6. Technical gotchas, all of these cost time on the Janet build

1. **Content must be visible by default.** The first build hid every section
   behind a scroll animation driven by IntersectionObserver. On a phone preview
   the observer never fired and the entire proposal rendered blank below the
   hero. Fix: content visible in CSS, animation added by JavaScript only, driven
   by element positions, with a timed failsafe. Always test with JavaScript
   disabled.
2. **Test on a real mobile viewport** with Playwright before sending. Chromium is
   preinstalled at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`. Check for horizontal overflow,
   broken images, console errors, and that any animated counters end on the right
   number rather than zero.
3. **Images can be extracted from the supplied PDF.** `pdfimages -all` pulled the
   Krindjabo ceremony photographs, the throne portrait and the Grammy photograph
   straight out of the Rolling Stone deck. No need to ask for re uploads.
   `pdftotext -layout` gets the copy.
4. **Comparison images can be composited.** The IShowSpeed and Michael Jackson
   side by side was built with Pillow from an AToure repository photo and a deck
   photo, labelled in brand colours.
5. **Serve media from the site's own origin.** Use site relative paths such as
   `/Media/...`, not raw.githubusercontent.com URLs. This matches the convention
   already adopted on the site and avoids rate limiting.
6. **For chat previews, embed images as base64.** The preview sandbox blocks
   external requests, so a page that looks broken in the chat preview may be
   perfectly fine live. Build a separate self contained preview file, and
   downscale the images so it stays light enough to open on a phone.

## 7. Git and deployment, read before publishing

1. **Always merge main into the working branch before merging back.** On this
   project the branch was six commits behind main, and merging as it stood would
   have deleted the Race Weekend post event report, its media and the partner
   logos. Check with `git log --oneline HEAD..origin/main` and
   `git diff --stat origin/main..HEAD` first. This is the single most dangerous
   step in the whole process.
2. **The website deploys from main via GitHub Pages** to atoureconsulting.com.
3. **Pages builds fail intermittently** with GitHub returning 429 on its own
   action downloads. It is not a content problem. Re trigger with an empty
   commit: `git commit --allow-empty -m "Re-trigger the Pages deployment"`.
4. **The website repository is unhealthy and this makes deploys fragile.** The
   git directory is 4.2 GB, with 848 MB of video across 30 tracked files, one
   file at 85.4 MB against GitHub's 100 MB hard limit, an 81 MB video stored
   twice in two locations, and a 73 MB source JPEG. Checkout alone takes over
   five minutes. Worth cleaning up separately.
5. **Confidential pages** carry `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`
   and stay out of `sitemap.xml`. They are still public to anyone holding the
   link, so treat the URL itself as the confidential item.

## 8. Questions to answer at the start of the next creator project

1. Which creator, and what is their genuine link to the country. The Janet
   proposal works because the Jackson family history in Côte d'Ivoire is real.
   Without a real hook the story section is just flattery.
2. Which country and cities.
3. Has the creator's team agreed, or is the deck intended to win them over.
4. What is the travelling party size and the trip length. Janet was modelled at
   fifteen people over seven days.
5. What is the finalised tour budget, since the base fee depends on it.
6. Who signs AToure's agreement.
7. What fixed date in the local calendar can anchor the timing argument.
8. Which institutions matter for this particular creator. The access network
   should be tailored, not copied wholesale.
